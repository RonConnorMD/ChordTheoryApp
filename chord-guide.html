How to Build Chords and Understand Inversions

A chord is a group of notes heard together. Learning how those notes are chosen—and how their order can change—makes chords easier to build and recognize.

Start with a three-note chord

A triad is built from a root, a third, and a fifth. The root gives the chord its name. The third helps determine whether the chord is major or minor, and the fifth completes the basic triad.

Build a major triad

A major triad uses scale degrees 1, 3, and 5. Starting on C, those notes are C, E, and G.

C major: C – E – G
Formula: 1 – 3 – 5

Build a minor triad

Compared with A major (A – C♯ – E), A minor lowers the third by one semitone, from C♯ to C. Its notes are A, C, and E.

A minor: A – C – E
Formula: 1 – ♭3 – 5

Understand chord inversions

An inversion keeps the chord's notes but changes which note is lowest. For C major, the notes remain C, E, and G:

  • Root position: C is lowest (C – E – G).
  • First inversion: E is lowest (E – G – C), written C/E.
  • Second inversion: G is lowest (G – C – E), written C/G.

The slash in a chord symbol such as C/E names the bass note. It means “a C major chord with E in the bass.”
